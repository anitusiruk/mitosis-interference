2. THE REFRAME (this session)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Paper title: "Capacity or Classifier? What Expansion Triggers Detect in Task-Free Continual
Adapter Learning"  (paper/main.tex, TMLR template, compiles with latexmk).

Thesis:
 (a) Proposition: at a label-novelty event, with untrained (zero) rows for new labels, the
     loss of an existing package = log|N| + log(1 + sum_o exp(z_o(x))/|N|); the excess depends
     on x only through OLD-label logits. So loss-spike triggers and fresh-module utility are
     label-novelty detectors, not capacity-need detectors.
 (b) Measured value of expansion (oracle expansion at true boundaries vs single package):
     HURTS in class-incremental (CIL), helps slightly in same-label domain shift (DIL),
     helps most under concept drift (labels flipped in a domain = positive control).
 (c) Common triggers fire at class arrivals (where expansion hurts) and miss drift.
 (d) Proposed LabelSurprise: mean over batch of [loss(x,y) - baseline_y], baseline_y = mean
     of last 20 batch-mean losses of label y under current package; needs >=3 prior records
     per label and >=4 qualifying examples, else undefined (no fire). Label-conditional
     version of DDM/ADWIN-style error drift detection. Related prior: Masud et al. 2011
     (novel class vs concept drift in stream mining) — cited; our novelty is showing adapter-
     expansion triggers conflate them and that it costs accuracy.
