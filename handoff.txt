1. THE PAPER'S STORY (current, evidence-backed)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title: "Capacity or Classifier? What Expansion Triggers Detect in Task-Free Continual Adapter
Learning" (consider adding "...and When Expansion Pays").

"Capacity-or-Classifier" framework = evaluation framework + findings (not a SOTA method):
 (1) Diagnostic (Prop. 1): when the current package's head has never trained on some batch labels
     (PACKAGE-level label novelty: new to the stream OR returning after a spawn), excess loss depends
     on x only through old-label logits => loss-spike and fresh-module-utility triggers are
     classifier-novelty detectors, not capacity-need detectors.
 (2) Value-of-expansion protocol: judge triggers by measured value (oracle expansion, count-matched
     random expansion) against TWO references (single package that keeps learning; first-session
     adaptation = SEMA/APER ablation reference), thresholds calibrated on i.i.d.-shuffled nulls.
 (3) Regime map: value of expansion depends on whether the learner can REFRESH its classifier
     statistics (exemplars) — exemplar-based: expansion hurts in CIL; exemplar-free (prototypes
     stored once, as in SEMA/EASE/APER): expansion at classifier novelty helps (dev; extension 2
     is testing it). Under drift: small/mixed; in vision depends on routing (proto_own).
 (4) LabelSurprise: label-conditional loss surprise (label-conditional DDM/DDM-OCI-style). Correct
     diagnostic of *established-label degradation*; never spawns in CIL/DIL. But detection != value:
     it hurts where expansion hurts (senti_conf) and is wrong in the exemplar-free regime.
