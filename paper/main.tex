\begin{abstract}
Dynamic-expansion continual learners add an adapter when a trigger---a loss spike, a representation shift, a prospective-forgetting estimate, or the utility of a fresh module---signals that existing capacity is inadequate. We ask what these triggers detect and when expanding is worth it. At the arrival of labels that the current module's classifier has never trained on, the excess loss depends on the input only through the old labels' logits, so loss-based triggers and one-step fresh-module utility are \emph{classifier-novelty} detectors. In a pre-registered study (ten text streams, three held out; DistilBERT, a held-out BERT backbone and ViT-B/16), every loss-spike and fresh-utility expansion in class-incremental streams fell at package-level label novelty. We then measure the value of expansion directly against two references, a single adapter that keeps learning and first-session adaptation. When the learner keeps exemplars from which its classifier statistics are refreshed, oracle expansion at the true boundaries costs 7--11 points in class-incremental text and CIFAR-100 streams (18 on ImageNet-R) relative to a single adapter, is neutral under domain shift and mixed under concept drift, although it beats first-session adaptation, the reference used in self-expansion ablations. When the learner is exemplar-free, so that a training adapter's prototypes go stale, oracle expansion beats the single adapter by 18--31 points in every class-incremental stream and seed (8 on ImageNet-R). A matched control on identical trajectories, in which stale and refreshed prototypes share items, coverage and aggregation, attributes most of this gain to feature age: refreshing the features alone lowers the value of expansion by 19--29 points, to between 2 and 5 points below the single adapter in text and CIFAR-100. Expansion with equal replay access still loses in the exemplar-based regime. Semantic drift compensation recovers part of the staleness in some streams but leaves expansion ahead in all of them, and a smaller interaction appears inside the official implementation of a published self-expanding method (SEMA), whose expansion decision showed no detectable advantage over never expanding. Expansion at classifier novelty therefore acts mainly as a remedy for stale classifier statistics rather than for missing capacity. Closed-loop runs on shift-free streams expose the mechanism once more: a fresh-module-utility trigger re-fires after its own spawns, because every new module makes all labels novel again; inheriting the classifier head removes the cascade. We recommend that expansion triggers be evaluated by the measured value of expansion against both reference points, not by boundary precision.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Parameter-efficient continual learning commonly grows a pool of adapters around a frozen pre-trained model \citep{zhou2024ease,wang2025sema,wei2025onlinelora}. When task identities are unavailable---the task-free setting \citep{aljundi2019taskfree}---the learner must itself decide \emph{when} to allocate a new module. Published decision rules monitor training-loss spikes or plateaus \citep{wei2025onlinelora,tathe2026lace}, distribution-shift descriptors of intermediate representations \citep{wang2025sema}, prospective forgetting after a virtual update \citep{julian2025cable}, or the hindrance a new task places on forward learning \citep{feng2024lw2g}. These signals are usually validated by \emph{boundary precision}---whether expansions coincide with the hidden task boundaries of a benchmark---or by ablations against a model that adapts only on the first task.

Boundary precision and the \emph{value} of expansion are different quantities. Most boundaries in standard benchmarks are class-incremental: new labels arrive with new inputs. A learner whose classifier can grow new output rows, on top of a pre-trained representation that already separates the new classes reasonably well, may need no new adapter at all. Whether a new adapter is nevertheless useful can depend on something other than capacity: in exemplar-free learners, class prototypes recorded under an adapter that keeps training become stale \citep{yu2020sdc}, and freezing that adapter---which is what expansion does---keeps them valid.

This paper asks what common expansion triggers detect, and when what they detect warrants expansion. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A diagnostic.} We show (Proposition~\ref{prop:novelty}) that at a package-level label-novelty event the excess loss of an existing module depends on the input only through its old-label logits, so loss-spike triggers and one-step fresh-module utility respond to classifier novelty, not to representational inadequacy. Empirically, every loss-spike and fresh-utility expansion in our class-incremental streams falls at such an event.
\item \textbf{A value-of-expansion protocol.} We measure the value of expansion with oracle expansion at the true boundaries and count-matched random timing, against two references---a single adapter that keeps learning with the same memory budget, and first-session adaptation \citep{panos2023fsa,zhou2024aper}---with trigger thresholds calibrated on shift-free nulls rather than tuned on outcomes.
\item \textbf{A regime map.} In the exemplar-based regime, expansion hurts in class-incremental streams yet beats first-session adaptation, so the conclusion of a self-expansion ablation depends on its reference point. In the exemplar-free regime the sign flips: expansion gains 18--31 points in class-incremental streams (8 on ImageNet-R), and first-segment adaptation comes within 7 points of oracle expansion (and beats it by 10 on ImageNet-R). A matched control isolates stale classifier statistics as the cause: on identical trajectories, prototypes that differ only in feature age lower the value of expansion by 19--29 points (11 on ImageNet-R), and with matched fresh features expansion no longer helps. Exemplar-free drift compensation, the standard remedy for stale prototypes, shrinks the value of expansion where it refreshes well but never removes it. Giving new packages access to the whole replay memory does not remove the harm of expansion with exemplars. The interaction replicates in vision (ViT-B/16, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R) and, smaller, inside SEMA's official implementation, where the expansion decision showed no detectable advantage over never expanding.
\item \textbf{A label-conditional detector, and why detection is not enough.} \ls{} compares each example's loss with the recent loss of its own label and ignores labels without history. It never expands at class arrivals and fires when established labels change, but its expansions help only where expansion helps.
\end{enumerate}
The study was pre-registered with frozen code hashes in five stages, uses the seed as the unit of analysis, and reports every failed hypothesis. We do not propose a method that competes with the state of the art; our learner is deliberately simple so that the expansion decision is the only moving part.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Expansion in continual learning.} Architectural approaches add capacity per task \citep{rusu2016pnn,yoon2018den} or on demand in task-free streams \citep{lee2020cndpm,ye2023sedem}. With pre-trained models, adapter pools are expanded per task \citep{zhou2024ease} or when a shift is detected: SEMA uses autoencoder-based representation descriptors \citep{wang2025sema}, Online-LoRA monitors loss plateaus \citep{wei2025onlinelora}, LACE expands on loss spikes relative to a recent-loss baseline \citep{tathe2026lace}, CABLE scores prospective forgetting after a virtual update \citep{julian2025cable}, and LW2G learns whether to grow from a hindrance metric \citep{feng2024lw2g}. These signals are validated by boundary precision---LACE reports that all of its expansions fall at domain boundaries---or by ablations that remove expansion while keeping first-session adaptation, as in SEMA. We stylise the signals of these families inside one learner, evaluate a LACE-style rule with its published threshold, and run SEMA's own code with the expansion decision varied.

\paragraph{Reference points for pre-trained-model continual learning.} Adapting a pre-trained model on the first session and freezing it is a strong replay-free baseline \citep{panos2023fsa,zhou2024aper}, and prototype classifiers on pre-trained features are strong in general \citep{rebuffi2017icarl,mai2021scr,mcdonnell2023ranpac}. Continual fine-tuning of a single model is competitive when representation learning is slowed and the classifier is re-aligned \citep{zhang2023slca}. In exemplar-free learning, the features of old classes drift while the model trains; semantic drift compensation estimates this drift from current data \citep{yu2020sdc,gomezvilla2024ldc}, and SSIAT keeps a single shared adapter with semantic-shift compensation \citep{tan2024ssiat}. We use these as the two references of the value-of-expansion protocol and as an exemplar-free alternative to expansion.

\paragraph{Classifier effects in class-incremental learning.} Output-layer bias toward recent classes is well documented \citep{wu2019bic,hou2019lucir,caccia2022ace}, and the factorisation of class-incremental prediction into task-identity and within-task prediction is established \citep{kim2022theoretical}. We use the corresponding loss identity as a diagnostic of expansion triggers; the identity itself is not new.

\paragraph{Drift and novelty detection in streams.} Error-rate drift detectors \citep{page1954cusum,gama2004ddm,bifet2007adwin} and novel-class detection under concept drift \citep{masud2011novel} distinguish concept change from class emergence in classical stream mining, and class-specific monitoring avoids the masking of drift by class-prior changes \citep{wang2013ddmoci}. \ls{} transfers this label-conditional view to the expansion decision.

\section{Setting and learner}
\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{Stream.} A task-free learner observes batches $(\mathbf{x}_t,\mathbf{y}_t)$, $t=1,\dots,T$, of 16 labelled examples. Segment identities are evaluation metadata and are never shown to the learner, except by the oracle reference policy.

\paragraph{Packages.} A frozen encoder $f$ (DistilBERT \citep{sanh2019distilbert}; BERT-base \citep{devlin2019bert} as held-out backbone; ViT-B/16 pre-trained on ImageNet-21k \citep{dosovitskiy2021vit} for vision) is shared. A package $k$ consists of LoRA factors \citep{hu2022lora} on query/value projections (rank 8) and a linear head over the global label space. Only the newest package trains; earlier packages are frozen, as in expand-and-freeze adapter pools \citep{zhou2024ease,wang2025sema}. A new package starts from zero-output LoRA and a zero head.

\paragraph{Training.} For every incoming batch the newest package takes two AdamW steps on the batch plus, in the exemplar-based regime, 16 items replayed from the memory items it owns, i.e.\ those observed while it was the newest package \citep{chaudhry2019tiny}, with cross-entropy over the labels observed so far.

\paragraph{Memory.} A single global reservoir \citep{vitter1985reservoir} of 512 examples is shared by the whole pool, so expansion never increases the memory budget. It does change replay access: a single package can rehearse the whole reservoir, whereas a newly spawned package rehearses only items observed since its creation. This is the usual expand-and-freeze design, but it means that expansion changes replay access as well as capacity and freezing; Section~\ref{sec:res-controls} reports expansion with global replay access.

\paragraph{Two regimes of classifier statistics.} Predictions use cosine prototypes; class scores are averaged over packages (equivalently, nearest-class-mean on the normalised concatenation of package features, as in EASE \citep{zhou2024ease}). In the \emph{exemplar-based} regime, prototypes are recomputed from memory under each package at prediction time, so they always match the current features. In the \emph{exemplar-free} regime (no replay, as in APER, EASE and SEMA), each package accumulates class means of features computed when the examples were observed and never recomputes them; a package spawned after a class was observed holds no prototype for it. Prototypes of a package that keeps training therefore go stale, while those of a frozen package stay valid. (Task-based methods compute prototypes once a task has ended; a task-free learner has no task end, so prototypes accumulate online. Drift compensation, Section~\ref{sec:triggers}, is the standard remedy for the resulting staleness.) Every exemplar-free run also reports the exemplar-based rule on the identical trajectory. The two rules differ in more than the age of the features: the refreshed rule uses the 512-item reservoir rather than every observed example, lets a package score classes observed before it was spawned, and averages over all packages rather than over those holding a prototype. To isolate feature age we add two matched rules on the same trajectories (Section~\ref{sec:res-controls}): both use the reservoir items, count an item only for the packages that existed when it was observed, and average over the packages holding a prototype; \emph{matched-stale} uses the features recorded at observation, \emph{matched-fresh} recomputes them under the current parameters.

\paragraph{Learner competence.} A single package reaches 85\% on the BANKING77 recurrence stream versus 65\% for training-free frozen-feature prototypes (Table~\ref{tab:main}). An earlier version of this project used a learner that reached only 16\%---below the training-free baseline---and we discarded its accuracy comparisons (Appendix~\ref{app:history}).

\section{What a loss signal measures at a label-novelty event}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Let $S$ be the labels that may be predicted, partitioned into labels $O$ that the current package has trained on with positive examples and labels $N$ in the current batch that it has not. $N$ contains labels new to the stream, but also labels seen before the package was created: in an expand-and-freeze pool, a returning label group is new to the package spawned since it was last seen. We call both \emph{package-level label novelty}. For an example with label $y$ in group $G(y)\in\{O,N\}$, and for any logits,
\begin{equation}
\ell(x,y) = \underbrace{-\log \textstyle\sum_{c\in G(y)} p(c\mid x)}_{\text{label-group term}}\;\underbrace{-\;\log p\big(y \mid x, G(y)\big)}_{\text{within-group term}} .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:novelty}
Suppose the head rows of labels in $N$ are zero, so $z_c(x)=0$ for $c\in N$. For $y\in N$ the loss of an existing package is
\[
\ell(x,y) = \log|N| \;+\; \log\Big(1+\tfrac{1}{|N|}\textstyle\sum_{o\in O} e^{z_o(x)}\Big),
\]
where the first term is the within-group term and the second is the label-group term. A fresh package with a zero head incurs $\log(|N|+|O|)$. Hence the advantage of the fresh package before any update (reuse loss minus fresh loss) is $\log\big((|N|+\sum_{o}e^{z_o(x)})/(|N|+|O|)\big)$, which is positive whenever the mean of $e^{z_o(x)}$ over old labels exceeds one, and which depends on $x$ only through the old-label logits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $z_c=0$ for $c\in N$, $p(y\mid x)=1/(|N|+\sum_o e^{z_o})$ and $p(y\mid x,N)=1/|N|$; the identity follows from \eqref{eq:decomp}. A zero head assigns $1/|S|$ to every label.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of the proposition.} The zero-row condition holds exactly for labels new to the stream: the training loss is restricted to labels already observed, so their rows have never received a gradient. It does not hold for returning labels. A package spawned after a label was observed trains that label's row with negative-only gradients, so the row is not zero when the label returns. The proposition also describes the state before any update; it does not by itself determine \textsc{FreshUtil}, which compares packages after one virtual update. We therefore use it for stream-level label novelty and treat returning labels empirically. In an exploratory measurement on BANKING-REC (oracle trajectories, ten seeds, identical to the saved runs; \texttt{experiments/tfcl\_return\_rows.py}), the deciding package scored returning labels 1.8--2.4 logits below the labels it had trained on, and the label-group term at returning examples was 3.1--3.7 nats (seed means), against 1.6--2.2 had their rows been zero. For a loss signal, returning labels thus look like new labels, more strongly so; this is an observation, not a consequence of the proposition.

The proposition is elementary, but its consequence for expansion triggers is direct. A loss-spike detector at a class arrival measures how confidently the old head predicts old labels, and a fresh-versus-reuse comparison before any update rewards discarding that confident head. After one virtual update the label-group term is still present but is no longer the only difference; in an earlier allocator built on a weaker learner, about 87\% of the fresh-package advantage at its spawn windows was already present before the update (Appendix~\ref{app:cau}). Neither signal asks whether the representation can separate the new labels---a question that a growing head and a prototype classifier answer without new adapter capacity. The label-group term is 36--52\% of the boundary loss in our class-incremental streams even after several steps of learning (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}).

\subsection{A label-conditional alternative}
\label{sec:surprise}
If label novelty is to be absorbed by the classifier, expansion should respond to evidence that an \emph{established} label's mapping has changed. Let $B_y$ be the mean of the last 20 batch-mean losses of label $y$ under the current package.
\begin{definition}[\ls]
For a batch, $\mathrm{LS} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{E}} \big(\ell(x_i,y_i) - B_{y_i}\big)$, where $\mathcal{E}$ contains the examples whose label has at least three prior batch-loss records under the current package. LS is undefined (cannot trigger) if $|\mathcal{E}|<4$.
\end{definition}
Newly introduced labels are excluded by construction, and while a label is being learned its loss falls, so LS is typically negative under class arrivals. Under concept drift, an established label's loss rises above its own history. LS is an error-based drift statistic \citep{gama2004ddm} computed per label. Two earlier variants failed during development (Appendix~\ref{app:history}).

\section{Triggers, references and protocol}
\label{sec:triggers}

\paragraph{Trigger statistics.} All triggers are computed on the newest package before it trains on the incoming batch. \textsc{LabelNovel}: fraction of batch labels never observed (a trivial reference). \textsc{LossZ}: $z$-score of the incoming loss against a 20-batch window (loss-spike triggers; \citealp{tathe2026lace,wei2025onlinelora}). \textsc{ReprZ}: $z$-score of a label-free diagonal-Mahalanobis feature distance (distribution-shift descriptors; \citealp{wang2025sema}). \textsc{InterfLogit}: increase in protected-memory cross-entropy after a virtual update (prospective forgetting; \citealp{julian2025cable}). \textsc{FreshUtil}: cross-fitted query-loss advantage of a fresh package over updating the current one after one virtual update (counterfactual utility; Appendix~\ref{app:cau}). Two further statistics, \textsc{InterfProto} and \textsc{ConflictZ}, are failed development proposals reported for completeness, and \ls{} is defined above. Virtual updates use exact snapshot/restore of parameters, optimiser moments and random states; a test verifies bitwise restoration.

\paragraph{Decision rule and calibration.} After the newest package has at least eight updates, a trigger spawns when its statistic exceeds its threshold on two consecutive batches; values in alarm are not added to baseline windows. Each threshold is the $0.99$-quantile of the statistic on an i.i.d.-shuffled version of the same stream (same examples, no shift) under the same backbone and regime, using only the development seed. This quantile calibration targets a common marginal exceedance rate and uses no outcome. It does not by itself equalise closed-loop false-spawn rates, which also depend on the two-consecutive-exceedance rule, the warm-up and the evolving baselines; we measure those rates on independent shuffled streams (Section~\ref{sec:res-controls}).

\paragraph{Streams.} Class-incremental (CIL): BANKING77 \citep{casanueva2020banking} class-group recurrence (A B A C B) and seven disjoint groups, CLINC150 \citep{larson2019clinc} (ten groups of 15), \emph{held-out} 20 Newsgroups \citep{lang1995newsweeder}, Split CIFAR-100 \citep{krizhevsky2009cifar} (ten groups of ten) and ImageNet-R \citep{hendrycks2021imagenetr} (ten groups of 20). Same-label domain shift (DIL): Amazon reviews \citep{keung2020marc} across domains, \emph{held-out} SST-2$\to$Yelp$\to$tweets$\to$IMDB \citep{socher2013sst,zhang2015charcnn,barbieri2020tweeteval,maas2011imdb}, and a BREEDS-style \citep{santurkar2021breeds} CIFAR-100 superclass stream in which domain $d$ contains the $d$-th subclass of every superclass. Concept drift: the Amazon and sentiment streams with some domains' labels flipped, the CIFAR superclass stream with two domains relabelled, and a natural label-semantics stream of TweetEval tasks over the same tweet population. Development examples come from training splits only.

\paragraph{Policies.} \emph{Frozen}: training-free prototypes. \emph{Single}: one package throughout. \emph{First-segment}: one package trained on the first segment only, then frozen, while memory and prototypes keep updating (first-session adaptation). \emph{Oracle}: spawn at every true segment change. \emph{Random}: the oracle's number of spawns at uniformly random steps. \emph{Triggers}: the statistics above.

\paragraph{Exemplar-free refresh.} Semantic drift compensation (SDC; \citealp{yu2020sdc}) moves each stored prototype $\mu_c$ of the training package by the kernel-weighted mean drift of the current batch, $\Delta_c=\sum_i w_{ic}(z_i^{\text{new}}-z_i^{\text{old}})/\sum_i w_{ic}$ with $w_{ic}=\exp(-\|z_i^{\text{old}}-\mu_c\|^2/2\sigma^2)$, on normalised embeddings with the published $\sigma=0.3$. We apply it after every update, because a task-free stream has no tasks. SDC changes no training computation and is reported as a further prototype rule on the same trajectories.

\paragraph{Statistics and pre-registration.} The study was pre-registered in five stages before any confirmation run of each stage (repository files \texttt{notes/tmlr\_*prereg.md}, with code hashes): the primary text study (seeds 2027--2036; BERT 2027--2031); a vision, baseline-dependence and natural-drift extension (vision seeds 2027--2034); the exemplar-free regime (text 2027--2036, vision 2027--2034); ImageNet-R plus drift compensation (5--6 seeds); and controls for feature age, replay access and closed-loop false spawns (Section~\ref{sec:res-controls}; seeds as in the corresponding earlier stages, five or three for false spawns). Development used seed 2026 only. The seed is the unit of analysis. We report paired seed-level mean differences with 95\% $t$-intervals and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm-corrected within each hypothesis family (for ImageNet-R, drift compensation and SEMA, with 3--6 seeds, a hypothesis is supported when its $t$-interval excludes zero on the predicted side, as pre-registered); deviations are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}.

\subsection{What the triggers detect}
\label{sec:res-detect}
\paragraph{Spawn attribution.} In the class-incremental streams without recurrence, every spawn of \textsc{LossZ} and \textsc{FreshUtil} fell within two batches after a batch containing a never-seen label (BANKING-CIL 55/55 and 55/55, CLINC 90/90 and 82/82, 20~Newsgroups 40/40 and 40/40, CIFAR-100 47/47 and 31/31; pooled over seeds). On the recurrence stream BANKING-REC, only 45\% and 50\% did, so this pre-registered hypothesis failed there. When a label group returns, its labels are new to the package spawned since they were last seen, although their rows are not zero (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}). In an analysis declared after the failure, 100\% of \textsc{LossZ} and \textsc{FreshUtil} spawns in every class-incremental stream, BANKING-REC included, fell within two batches of package-level label novelty, against a base rate of 15--30\% of eligible batches.

\paragraph{A published threshold.} The LACE-style rule (loss above $2.5\times$ its recent mean; \citealp{tathe2026lace}), applied open-loop to single-package traces (five seeds), reproduces the high boundary precision reported for it: in BANKING-CIL, 20~Newsgroups and CIFAR-100 every firing fell within two batches of a segment change (27/27, 20/20, 27/27), each also a batch with a never-seen label, and on BANKING-REC 5/5 firings did. On CLINC, 32 of 52 firings did. In the domain-shift and drift streams it fired rarely (2--12 times for 12--20 changes) and never near label novelty, which those streams lack; there, 2/8 to 12/12 firings were near a change. High boundary precision in class-incremental streams is therefore what a loss-ratio rule delivers on its own, consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:novelty}; it does not tell whether the resulting expansion pays (Section~\ref{sec:res-exemplar}).

\subsection{The value of expansion with exemplars}
\label{sec:res-exemplar}
\paragraph{Class-incremental: expansion hurts against a learner that keeps training\ldots} Oracle expansion at the true boundaries reduced final accuracy relative to a single package by 8.2 points on BANKING-REC, 11.4 on BANKING-CIL, 9.9 on CLINC and 7.1 on held-out 20~Newsgroups (every seed), and 8.9 [7.9, 10.0] on CIFAR-100 with ViT-B/16 (every seed of eight). Count-matched random-timing expansion was also harmful, and on BANKING-REC less so than the oracle (79.2\% vs.\ 77.1\%).

\paragraph{\ldots but helps against first-session adaptation.} The same oracle pools beat the first-segment policy by 7.2, 5.5 and 6.9 points (every seed) and by 0.9 points on 20 Newsgroups (n.s.), and by 16.6 [13.4, 19.8] on CIFAR-100 (every seed). A self-expansion ablation of the usual form would therefore conclude that expansion helps, in exactly the setting where it costs 7--11 points against a single package that keeps learning, which itself beats first-session adaptation by 15--17 points (Figure~\ref{fig:regime}b).

\paragraph{Held-out backbone.} With BERT-base (five seeds; declared secondary), the harm of expansion replicated in every class-incremental stream and every seed: oracle $-$ single was $-8.5$ [$-9.7$, $-7.3$] on BANKING-REC, $-9.7$ [$-11.5$, $-8.0$] on BANKING-CIL, $-7.9$ [$-9.4$, $-6.4$] on CLINC and $-5.5$ [$-6.4$, $-4.6$] on 20 Newsgroups, and \textsc{LossZ} lost 6.2--7.8 points. With five seeds the smallest attainable Wilcoxon $p$ is 0.0625, so no contrast passes the Holm criterion; the $t$-intervals exclude zero. On the drift streams expansion did not help BERT either ($-1.3$, $-2.1$ and $-12.7$ points), and \ls{} again spawned no package in any class-incremental or domain-shift stream.

\paragraph{Without rehearsal.} Removing replay from the training loss (memory still feeds the prototypes) reduced the harm of expansion: oracle$-$single was $-4.3$ on BANKING-REC (significant) and $-3.3$, $-0.6$, $-1.2$ on BANKING-CIL, CLINC and 20~Newsgroups (n.s.), and $+1.4$ (n.s.) on CIFAR-100, so our pre-registered prediction that the harm does not depend on rehearsal was mostly not supported. The difference-in-differences (without minus with replay) was $+3.9$ to $+10.3$ points in every class-incremental stream (significant, every seed of CIFAR-100).

\paragraph{Domain shift and drift.} Under same-label domain shift, oracle expansion was within half a point of the single package. Under concept drift its value was mixed: $+1.9$ points [0.5, 3.3] on Amazon-DILCONF, $+0.8$ [$-2.1$, 3.8] (n.s.) on Amazon-CONFLICT, and $-17.5$ [$-20.5$, $-14.5$] on the held-out sentiment drift stream, the opposite of our pre-registered prediction. In that stream the domains are distinguishable, a single package trained with replay learns the domain-conditional mapping (82\% on the flipped Yelp segment at the end), and frozen packages trained on the unflipped mapping out-vote the correct package under prototype averaging (16\%). On the natural annotation-drift stream (TweetEval tasks sharing labels; descriptive), oracle expansion was $-1.1$ points [$-2.2$, 0.1] and no trigger differed from the single package by more than 0.3 points. On CIFAR-100 domain shift and label-flip drift, oracle expansion lost 2.5 and 13.2 points under prototype averaging (every seed), but \emph{gained} 2.7 [1.7, 3.8] and 4.8 [4.1, 5.5] points under owned-prototype routing (every seed; the drift contrast was added by a dated addendum before any vision confirmation run, after development showed the same reversal). Under drift, whether expansion helps thus depends on how packages are combined at inference, a third axis next to the reference point and the refreshability of classifier statistics.

\subsection{The value of expansion without exemplars}
\label{sec:res-ef}
With exemplar-free prototypes, the sign of the value of expansion reverses (ten seeds per stream, Holm correction within each family). Oracle expansion beat the single package in all four class-incremental text streams, by 24.0 points [21.3, 26.8] on BANKING-REC, 29.8 [25.2, 34.4] on BANKING-CIL, 28.1 [22.5, 33.7] on CLINC-CIL and 18.4 [12.1, 24.7] on 20 Newsgroups, winning every seed (R1). Within the same runs, the difference between the exemplar-free and the exemplar-based value of expansion was 19.6--33.1 points (R2, every seed). The training trajectories are identical, so this difference comes from the prototype rules; since those differ in sample, coverage and aggregation as well as feature age, Section~\ref{sec:res-controls} isolates feature age. With refreshed prototypes the value of expansion was $-4.3$ [$-6.0$, $-2.6$] on BANKING-REC and $-3.3$, $-0.6$, $-1.2$ (intervals including zero) on the other three streams, so refreshing removes the benefit of expansion rather than reliably reversing it. The boundary-detecting \textsc{LossZ} trigger, which lost to the single package in the exemplar-based regime, beat \ls{} by 18.0--29.1 points (R3) and matched oracle expansion to within 0.9 points; \ls{}, which spawns essentially no package, was indistinguishable from the single package. Oracle placement beat random placement of the same number of packages by 6.1--27.4 points (R4), and the first-segment policy came within 4.5 points of oracle expansion (it beat oracle by 1.6 points on BANKING-CIL and 0.6 on 20 Newsgroups, n.s.). Under same-label domain shift in text every policy stayed within 1.7 points of the single package; under concept drift, expansion gained 3.4--4.4 points on Amazon-DILCONF and was mixed on Sentiment-CONF. With ViT on CIFAR-100 (eight seeds) the reversal was as large: oracle expansion beat the single package by 31.4 points [27.0, 35.8] (R1), the within-run difference-in-differences was 30.0 [26.0, 34.0] (R2), and \textsc{LossZ} beat \ls{} by 31.0 [27.1, 35.0] (R3), every seed. Placement mattered less there: random placement of the same number of packages also gained 27.4 points, and oracle $-$ random (R4) was $+3.9$ [$-4.7$, 12.6], not significant. On CIFAR-100 domain shift and drift, exemplar-free expansion gained 0.9 and 2.6 points.

\paragraph{Refreshing without exemplars.} If expansion helps exemplar-free learners only because a single package's stored prototypes go stale, an exemplar-free refresh should recover the single package and shrink the value of expansion (five seeds per stream). Drift compensation refreshed the single package in three of five streams (S1: $+19.6$ points [13.2, 26.0] on BANKING-REC, $+10.8$ [4.2, 17.3] on 20 Newsgroups, $+5.4$ [0.7, 10.0] on BANKING-CIL) but \emph{lowered} it on CLINC-CIL ($-8.6$ [$-17.0$, $-0.1$]) and CIFAR-CIL ($-19.8$ [$-22.2$, $-17.4$]), the two streams flagged as at risk in development. It shrank the value of expansion only where it refreshed well (S2 supported on BANKING-REC, $-18.6$ points, and 20 Newsgroups, $-8.9$); on CIFAR-CIL the value of expansion grew by 20.7 points. Whatever the rule, oracle expansion still beat the single package in every stream and every seed: by 7.4--49.4 points under SDC ($\sigma=0.3$), 11.0--51.0 under $\sigma=0.2$, and 9.5--49.8 under a uniform mean shift. Published exemplar-free refresh therefore repairs part of the staleness on text streams with small drift but does not remove the value of freezing; the first-segment policy remained within 7.4 points of oracle expansion under every rule (Appendix~\ref{app:ext4}).

\subsection{A canonical benchmark and a published system}
\label{sec:res-external}
\paragraph{ImageNet-R.} On the standard pre-trained-model benchmark (200 classes in ten groups of 20; six seeds), both regimes reproduce. With exemplar-refreshed prototypes, oracle expansion lost 18.3 points [16.2, 20.3] to the single package (I1) yet beat first-session adaptation by 5.5 [4.5, 6.6] (I2), every seed. Without exemplars the sign reversed: oracle expansion beat the single package by 8.1 points [3.8, 12.4] (I3), and within the same runs the value of expansion was 16.4 points [15.2, 17.7] higher under exemplar-free than under exemplar-refreshed prototypes (I4). Drift compensation did not rescue the single package here either: it lowered its accuracy by 13.6 points (every seed), and oracle $-$ single grew to $+22.1$ points under SDC. As on CIFAR-CIL, the exemplar-free first-segment policy was the best policy, beating oracle expansion by 9.6 points [6.8, 12.3]: once statistics cannot be refreshed, freezing early beats adapting further.

\paragraph{SEMA.} We ran SEMA's official implementation with its published CIFAR-100 configuration and varied only the expansion decision: the official rule; never expanding (adapters frozen after the first task, the reference of SEMA's own ablation); and never expanding while the adapters keep training on every task (the single package). Each run was scored after every task with SEMA's linear head, with exemplar-free class means recorded at the end of each task, and with class means recomputed from 20 stored exemplars per class (three seeds; pre-registered). SEMA expanded only in the last two blocks, adding 9--10 adapters to block 12 and 1--2 to block 11. Its final accuracy did not differ detectably from never expanding under any rule (linear head 86.4\% vs.\ 86.2\%, $+0.2$ points [$-1.3$, 1.8]; all mean differences within 0.6 points), so in our runs the expansion decision showed no statistically resolved advantage over first-session adaptation, the reference of SEMA's own ablation; with three seeds this is not evidence of equivalence. Our prediction that expansion beats the single package under SEMA's own exemplar-free linear head was not supported ($+0.6$ points [$-1.4$, 2.7]; E1). The single package lost nothing to SEMA without refresh but beat it by 1.8 points [0.1, 3.5] with exemplar-refreshed class means (E3, every seed). The pre-registered interaction (E2: the linear-head contrast minus the refreshed-NCM contrast) was 2.4 points [1.6, 3.3], every seed; because it also changes the classifier type, we add a comparison of the two NCM rules (exemplar-free class means at task end versus means recomputed from 20 exemplars per class; not pre-registered), which gives $+2.15$ points [1.89, 2.41], every seed. The two NCM rules still differ in sample support (all training data of a task versus 20 exemplars per class). In SEMA's configuration (adapters only in the last three of twelve blocks, five epochs per task), the single package's class means went only slightly stale: its accuracy with exemplar-free class means was 1.6--2.2 points below that with means recomputed from all training data. The exemplar-free advantage of freezing was therefore small, while the cost of freezing when statistics can be refreshed persisted.

\subsection{Closed-loop triggers and \ls{}}
\label{sec:res-closed}
Triggers that detect class boundaries spawned 3--5 packages per class-incremental stream and lost 4--10 points relative to the single package in the exemplar-based regime. \ls{} spawned essentially no package in any class-incremental or domain-shift stream, and so matched the single package (non-inferior in all seven), while on the drift streams it spawned 4--5 packages and tracked the oracle: $+2.1$ [0.9, 3.3] on Amazon-DILCONF, $+1.0$ (n.s.) on Amazon-CONFLICT and $-16.7$ on the held-out sentiment drift stream, where expansion itself is harmful. In the exemplar-free regime the ranking reversed: \textsc{LossZ} beat \ls{} by 18.0--29.1 points in every class-incremental text stream and seed, and matched oracle expansion to within 0.9 points. The pattern held with BERT-base (\textsc{LossZ} $-6.2$ to $-7.8$ points against the single package with exemplars; \ls{} spawned nothing in class-incremental and domain-shift streams) and with ViT on CIFAR-100: the four boundary-detecting triggers lost 3.0--5.5 points in the class-incremental stream, where \ls{} spawned nothing, and on the label-flip drift stream \ls{} spawned and tracked the oracle ($-12.1$ vs.\ $-13.2$ under prototype averaging). The one failure of specificity was CIFAR-100 domain shift, where \ls{} spawned and lost 2.4 points [1.4, 3.4], failing the pre-registered non-inferiority margin.

\subsection{Controls: feature age, replay access and false spawns}
\label{sec:res-controls}
\input{tables/controls_table.tex}
A further pre-registered stage (extension 6, frozen before its runs) tests three alternative explanations (Table~\ref{tab:controls}).

\paragraph{Feature age.} The stored and refreshed prototype rules differ in sample, package coverage and aggregation as well as in feature age (Section~\ref{sec:setting}). The matched rules differ \emph{only} in feature age. A single package lost 14.3--36.9 points when its prototypes were matched-stale rather than matched-fresh (M1, every seed of every class-incremental stream; Holm $p=0.0098$ for each of the five streams with eight or more seeds), and refreshing only the feature age reduced the value of expansion by 19.1--29.3 points in text and CIFAR-100 and by 10.9 on ImageNet-R (M2, every seed; Table~\ref{tab:controls}). Under matched-fresh prototypes, oracle expansion was 1.7--5.3 points \emph{below} the single package in text and CIFAR-100 (interval excluding zero in four of the five streams) and 14.3 below on ImageNet-R. Telescoping the stored-to-refreshed contrast through the matched rules attributes 75--98\% of it to feature age in text and CIFAR-100 (e.g.\ CLINC: sample 8.4, feature age 21.4, coverage and aggregation $-1.2$ points); the difference between the stored and refreshed values of expansion is therefore mainly a staleness effect. ImageNet-R is the exception: there the reservoir holds about 2.5 examples per class, and restricting the stored prototypes to the reservoir sample already removed the exemplar-free gain of expansion ($-3.5$ [$-7.7$, 0.8]); sample support and feature age contributed about equally (11.5 and 10.9 points).

\paragraph{Replay access.} With owned replay, a new package cannot rehearse items observed under its predecessors. Giving every package the whole reservoir changed little in text: oracle expansion with global replay still lost 6.3--10.9 points to the single package (G1, every seed; Holm $p=0.0098$ in each class-incremental stream), recovering 4--12\% of the harm of owned-replay expansion. Replay access mattered more in vision: it recovered 74\% of the harm on CIFAR-100 and 30\% on ImageNet-R, yet expansion still lost 2.3 and 12.8 points, every seed. On the held-out sentiment drift stream, global replay recovered 23\% ($-13.5$ vs.\ $-17.5$ points). The harm of expansion in the exemplar-based regime is therefore not an artefact of replay access, although in vision the replay design accounts for much of its size.

\paragraph{Closed-loop false spawns.} We ran every trigger closed-loop with its confirmation threshold on independently shuffled versions of the streams (no shift, so every spawn is false; five seeds per text stream, three per vision stream, seeds the thresholds never saw). \textsc{LabelNovel}, \textsc{LossZ}, \ls{}, \textsc{ReprZ} and \textsc{InterfLogit} spawned 0.07, 0.12, 0.02, 0.42 and 0.20 false packages per run on average (at most 1.4 per 100 eligible batches in any stream; Appendix~\ref{app:ext6}); without exemplars, \textsc{LossZ} and \ls{} spawned 0.25 and 0.10. \textsc{FreshUtil} was the exception. It spawned 41 packages in every CLINC run, about one at every window in which a new package became eligible, 17.7 per CIFAR-100 run (three of three seeds) and 4.8--5.6 per BANKING run (two or three of five seeds), and none on 20 Newsgroups or the domain-shift and drift streams. This runaway is the mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} acting on the trigger's own output: each spawn creates a package to which every label is new, so the next window shows package-level label novelty again, and the cascade continues where a batch holds many labels the new package has not yet trained (CLINC has 150 labels, CIFAR-100 100, BANKING 77, 20 Newsgroups 20). In an exploratory test declared before it was run, letting each new package inherit its predecessor's classifier head, so that spawning creates no untrained rows, reduced the false spawns from 41 to 1.4 per CLINC run and from 5.6 to 0.4 per BANKING-CIL run. Quantile calibration on a shift-free null thus gave low closed-loop false-spawn rates for five triggers but not for \textsc{FreshUtil}, whose specificity therefore depends on the stream. On the real streams it did not run away (9.2 packages on CLINC, against 10 for the oracle, and 4.9 on CIFAR-100; Table~\ref{tab:main}), but its spawns there cannot be read as evidence of detected shift.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

\paragraph{What expansion buys.} Expanding at a class arrival freezes the package whose features the stored class statistics were computed under. When those statistics are recomputed from exemplars, freezing only stops further learning, and a single package that keeps learning wins by 5--18 points across three backbones. When they cannot be recomputed, freezing keeps them valid, and expansion wins by 8--31 points. The matched control attributes most of this difference to feature age (75--98\% in text and CIFAR-100; about half on ImageNet-R, where sample support matters as much), so in our streams this property of the learner, more than the trigger or the stream, decided whether expansion at class arrivals paid; a smaller interaction appears in SEMA's own code. Refreshing the statistics within the same exemplar-free trajectories made the value of expansion negative on BANKING-REC and ImageNet-R and indistinguishable from zero elsewhere (CIFAR-100: $+1.3$ [$-0.4$, 3.1]); refreshing removes the benefit of expansion rather than reliably reversing it. Three remedies for stale statistics compete with expansion. Exemplar refresh removes the staleness but needs stored data. Exemplar-free drift compensation repaired part of it on text streams with small drift and failed on CLINC, CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-R. Freezing early, as first-session adaptation does, came within 7 points of oracle expansion in every class-incremental stream and beat it on ImageNet-R, without any trigger. For exemplar-free learners, therefore, the relevant comparison is not ``expand or not'' but ``expand at boundaries or freeze after the first segment''. In our streams the gap between the two was small next to the gap between either and a single package that keeps training.

\paragraph{Scope.} Our encoders are small to medium, our streams are short (80--500 batches), and four of five drift streams use synthetic relabelling. Our triggers are stylised versions of published signals inside a common learner; the SEMA experiment checks one complete system, and we make no claim about reported benchmark numbers. The value of expansion also depends on the inference rule: routing each input to the package whose own prototypes fit best reverses the sign of the value of expansion under domain shift and drift in vision (Section~\ref{sec:res-exemplar}).

\paragraph{Calibration.} Thresholds are set on an i.i.d.-shuffled version of the same stream. This puts the triggers' statistics on a common quantile scale without using outcomes, but it is not available to a deployed learner, and the resulting closed-loop false-spawn rates differ across triggers (Section~\ref{sec:res-controls}). The conclusion that loss-type triggers fire at label arrivals does not depend on the threshold, since their AUROC for class arrivals is near one.

\paragraph{What \ls{} detects.} \ls{} responds to an increase in the loss of established labels. Concept drift produces this, but so does a within-label shift of $P(x\mid y)$, such as a new subpopulation under an existing superclass. It is therefore a detector of \emph{established-label degradation}, not of concept drift per se.

\paragraph{Training budget of the references.} Our first-segment policy trains for the same number of steps per batch as every other policy; SEMA and APER adapt for full epochs on the first task. The SEMA experiment uses SEMA's own budget.

\paragraph{Recommendations.} When an expansion trigger is proposed, its evaluation should include (i) the measured value of expansion in the evaluated regime against a single module that keeps learning with the same memory budget, and against first-session adaptation; (ii) a statement of whether classifier statistics are refreshed, since this decides the sign of the value of expansion at class arrivals; (iii) separation of label novelty from changes in established labels, since boundary precision on class-incremental benchmarks is achieved by label-novelty detection alone; (iv) thresholds calibrated on shift-free nulls rather than tuned on outcomes; and (v) closed-loop false-spawn rates on shift-free streams, since a statistic calibrated open-loop can respond to the novelty its own spawns create (Section~\ref{sec:res-controls}).

\section{Conclusion}
Loss-spike and fresh-module-utility triggers detect classifier novelty: in class-incremental streams they fire where labels new to the current module arrive, which also explains their high boundary precision. Whether expanding there is worth it depends on whether the learner can refresh its classifier statistics. With exemplars, expansion costs accuracy against a single adapter that keeps learning, yet beats first-session adaptation, so a self-expansion ablation against the latter alone overstates its value. Without exemplars, expansion is a large gain because it keeps prototypes valid: a matched control shows that refreshing only the age of the features removes most of the gain, and freezing after the first segment achieves most of it without any trigger. These results held on text and vision backbones, ten-seed pre-registered contrasts, a held-out backbone and a published method's own code. We recommend that expansion triggers be evaluated by the measured value of expansion against both references, in a stated classifier-statistics regime, rather than by boundary precision.

\section{Development history and failed candidates}
\label{app:history}
This project began as the design of a task-free allocator (Appendix~\ref{app:cau}). Its learner took a single AdamW step per 16-example batch into a 77-way head and reached about 16\% final accuracy on the BANKING recurrence stream with label-free routing (22\% with a learned router), whereas training-free frozen-feature prototypes reached 71\% (logistic probe on frozen features: 86\%). We therefore discarded all of its accuracy comparisons and built the learner of Section~\ref{sec:setting}. All development of the new testbed used seed 2026 only:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textsc{InterfProto} (head-equalised prospective interference) was our first proposal. On the seed-2026 shadow traces it did not detect concept drift (AUROC 0.54 and 0.22 on the two development drift streams).
\item \textsc{ConflictZ} v1 restricted the loss $z$-score to examples with previously observed labels. It detected class-incremental boundaries (AUROC 1.00) because labels observed one batch earlier are still untrained. v2 required labels to be first observed at least eight batches earlier; it still fired in class-incremental streams, because newly learned labels have higher loss than long-established ones. This motivated the label-conditional baseline of \ls{}.
\item The first closed-loop pilot added alarmed values to the $z$-score baseline windows, so a genuine spike inflated its own baseline. Excluding alarmed values applies identically to all $z$-type triggers.
\item A slower single learner (learning rate $10^{-4}$) did not reduce exemplar-free staleness in development (BANKING-CIL: 13.1\% vs.\ 26.2\% at $10^{-3}$) and was not carried into the confirmation.
\end{enumerate}
The thresholds, learner hyperparameters (learning rate $10^{-3}$, two steps per batch, replay 16, memory 512) and all trigger constants were fixed before the confirmation study.

\section{Case study: an exact counterfactual allocator}
\label{app:cau}
Before this study, we developed and audited an allocator, CAU (counterfactual action utility), that compares an exact, optimiser-faithful one-step update of each existing package and of a fresh classifier-bearing package against a common no-update reference on cross-fitted query halves, subject to a separate protected-memory feasibility check, and spawns after two consecutive windows of positive fresh utility. On a BANKING77 class-group recurrence stream (A B A C B), CAU spawned exactly at the arrivals of the B and C label groups in all ten seed/order development trajectories. A \emph{head-only} variant---identical, except that LoRA outputs are held at zero and only the classifier trains---made identical allocation decisions in all ten trajectories. At the original spawn windows, about 87\% of the fresh-versus-best-reuse utility advantage was already present before the prospective update, i.e.\ it reflected the replacement of the old classifier rather than learning. These observations, made with the weaker learner of Appendix~\ref{app:history} and reported only for decisions, motivated Proposition~\ref{prop:novelty} and the present study.
